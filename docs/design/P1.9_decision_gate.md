## The two routes

### Route 1 — external benchmark: `fmfi-compbio/plasgraph2-datasets`

Provides what no other candidate does: **872 real assembly graphs** with coverage tags and
a per-contig truth of `chromosome` / `plasmid` / `ambiguous` / `unlabeled` across **53
species**, plus short- and long-read accessions for all 436 samples.

**Gate 1 — licence.** `NOT DOCUMENTED`. Resolve before any download. Options, in order of
preference: (a) owner confirms acceptable-use terms; (b) rebuild the labels independently
from the public NCBI/ENA accessions and the documented `minimap2` rules, which are fully
specified in the paper; (c) drop the route. **Contacting the authors is not authorised.**

**Gate 2 — independence.** Cross-check the 18 *E. coli* samples in this corpus against the
53 *E. coli* isolates used in P1.8B at accession level. Any overlapping isolate must be
removed, or the *E. coli* stratum must be excluded from primary analysis. Both corpora
trace to the same author group, so this is a live risk, currently `CANNOT DETERMINE`.

Additional required control: cluster by **source study** before splitting. Sixteen source
studies contribute, dominated by Shaw2021 (152) and Robertson2018 (120, the MOB-suite
corpus).

### Route 2 — controlled benchmark from ~385 complete genomes

Specified in `P1.9_controlled_benchmark_protocol_draft.yaml`. Recommended design is
**read simulation followed by assembly with graph retention**, with sequence fragmentation
retained as a graph-free control and two assemblers for sensitivity.

Its unique contribution is the **mobile-chromosomal hard-negative layer** — IS elements,
transposons, integrons, prophages, integrated plasmid segments and chromosomal conjugation
genes. No public candidate provides this, and it is the class that dominates current
Model 1 false positives. Without it, any benchmark will overstate PPV.

---

## Why hybrid is the right answer

The two routes fail in **opposite** directions, which is exactly why both are needed:

- The public corpus is **real** but its independence from our prior work is unproven and its
  hard negatives are unlabelled.
- The controlled corpus has **perfect** provenance, leakage control and hard negatives, but
  is simulated, so it carries simulator and assembler dependence.

A PPV ≥ 95% claim resting on simulation alone would not be credible. A claim resting on a
corpus that may share isolates with our own prior evaluation would not be independent.
Together they support a defensible claim: **the controlled benchmark for mechanism and hard
negatives, the external corpus for real-data confirmation.**
