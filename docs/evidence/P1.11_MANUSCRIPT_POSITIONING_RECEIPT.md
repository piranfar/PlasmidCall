## Prior art — the benchmark component is not novel, and the framing must change

Teixeira M, Souque C, Worby CJ, Shea T, Commins N, Smith JT, Miklos AM, Abeel T, Earl AM, Manson AL.
*Circling in on plasmids: benchmarking plasmid detection and reconstruction tools for short-read data
from diverse species.* Briefings in Bioinformatics 2025;26(6):bbaf589. doi:10.1093/bib/bbaf589.
Preprint doi:10.1101/2025.07.28.667252, 2025-08-05.

That study benchmarked 12 plasmid-detection tools and four reconstruction tools across
Enterobacterales and *Enterococcus*, and found **Plasmer, PlasmidEC, PlaScope and gplas2** to be the
highest scoring. It precedes this work and covers more species.

**Consequence for positioning.** The claim "we benchmarked twelve tools" is no longer a novel
contribution and must not be presented as one. The tool comparison here is **confirmatory of prior
work**: the four highest-F1 comparators are PlaScope (0.9278), gplas2 (0.9038), PlasmidEC (0.8925)
and Plasmer (0.8773) — the same four, on the disjoint 79-isolate P1.11 cohort.

**A provenance fact that must be disclosed.** The development collection behind PlasmidCall v1.1,
DEV_250, **is** the Teixeira et al. isolate set: all 250 BioSample accessions match exactly
(`P1.9_development_accession_recovery_receipt_v2.md`). Consequences: the development set must be
cited as theirs; v1.1's E. coli evaluation is a species-shifted test *on their collection*; and the
tool-ranking agreement is cross-cohort corroboration, **not** fully independent replication, since
the tool panel is shared and v1.1 was built on their data. The P1.11 cohort itself is disjoint from
DEV_250 by identifier (0 shared BioSamples) and by sequence (max ANI 99.48 %, below the frozen
99.5 % threshold), and no P1.11 truth or metadata entered model development.

**The claim that survives, and should now lead.** No published benchmark asks which tools clear a
declared precision floor for deployment. On this cohort exactly one of twelve does — PlasmidFinder,
PPV 0.9831 at recall 0.2009 — and PlasmidCall v1.2-General clears the same floor at 3.9× that
recall. Together with prospective freezing (sealed cohort, thresholds declared before truth access,
predictions hashed before truth acquisition, analysis scripts that refuse to run unless they
re-derive the confirmatory table exactly), that is the contribution specific to this work, alongside
ARG localisation yield, empirical calibration, copy-number stratification and replicon-level
recovery.

**Where the prior benchmark is stronger, stated plainly.** It spans Enterobacterales *and*
enterococci; the confirmatory cohort here is single-species. It also covers plasmid reconstruction,
which is out of scope here. Both are limitations of this work.

**Venue consequence.** *Briefings in Bioinformatics* is no longer an appropriate target, having
published the competing benchmark. Any submission framed primarily as a twelve-tool benchmark should
expect to be rejected on novelty; the submission must lead with the precision-floor result and the
prospective-validation methodology.

## Biological yield — EXPLORATORY

Evaluable set: 218 ARG-bearing contigs ≥1 kb with a definitive truth label, across 63 isolates
(166 truth-plasmid, 52 truth-chromosome), from 271 ARG-bearing contigs in the frozen table.

| Configuration | Plasmid-borne ARG contigs recovered | Chromosomal ARG mislocalised |
|---|---|---|
| **v1.2-General @0.9285** | **148 / 166 (89.2 %)** | 18 / 52 |
| Router | **92 / 166 (55.4 %)** | 3 / 52 |

**The router loses 56 ARG–plasmid localisations relative to v1.2-General.**

Mechanism, verified exactly: the frozen router routes **all and only** ARG-bearing contigs to v1.1
— 271 routed, 271 ARG-bearing, sets identical, router call equals the v1.1 call in 271/271 rows.
On the population most relevant to surveillance the router therefore *is* v1.1, which is far more
conservative.

**Positioning:** the router is **retained as a secondary high-precision operating strategy**
(whole-cohort PPV 0.9801 [0.9602, 0.9946] vs 0.9662). It is **not recommended for ARG–plasmid
surveillance**, and it is **not altered post hoc** — any change would require its own prospective
amendment on a new cohort.

This is a localisation and surveillance-yield analysis. **No claim of biological novelty is made.**

## Mechanistic account of the recall figure — EXPLORATORY

The confirmatory recall of 0.7792 is not diffuse error. It concentrates in two identifiable places,
which strengthens rather than qualifies the principal claim:

- **Copy number.** Recall rises monotonically with a per-isolate plasmid-copy-number proxy
  (contig depth ÷ median depth of that isolate's own ≥10 kb chromosomal contigs): 0.536 below 0.5×,
  0.688 at ~single copy, 0.848 at 1.5–4×, 0.882 at 4–15×, and **0.988 at ≥15×**. Missed plasmid
  contigs have a median proxy of 1.176 against 1.67 for recovered ones. The misses are
  single-copy plasmid contigs — those least distinguishable from chromosome by any available signal.
- **Replicon size.** Contig-level recall declines from 0.933 (<10 kb replicons) to 0.667 (≥200 kb,
  6 replicons, indicative only), consistent with large extrachromosomal replicons converging toward
  chromosomal composition as they grow.

**Replicon-level recovery.** Across 329 plasmid replicons, **94.8 % had at least one contig
recovered**, 73.3 % were recovered completely, 90.0 % had ≥50 % of assembled bp recovered, and 17
were missed entirely. This is the unit surveillance acts on, since one flagged contig triggers
follow-up.

**Constraints on how these may be used.** All three are EXPLORATORY and post-hoc; they were
produced after the accepted post-truth set was first frozen. The 0.948 replicon-level figure
**must not** be presented as the prespecified recall estimand — the confirmatory recall remains
0.7792 [0.7353, 0.8239] per contig — and must always appear beside the stricter 0.733
complete-recovery figure. "At least one contig recovered" is mechanically easier to satisfy for
replicons split across many contigs (multi-contig replicons: 0.992 any-contig against 0.455
complete), and replicon assignment depends on `best_plasmid_hit`, so replicons sharing sequence may
merge or split relative to biology.

regenerated. The independence threshold (ANI ≥ 0.995) was **reused unchanged**, not reselected.

- Maximum ANI between any two retained isolates: 99.45 % — internally distinct as well.
- **Performance is stable across distance bands:** 98.0–<99.0 % (30 isolates, 2,970 contigs)
  PPV 0.9514 / recall 0.7686; 99.0–<99.5 % (49 isolates, 4,274 contigs) PPV 0.9752 / recall 0.7856.

skani `--min-af 15` reporting floor means a missing pair would be a lower bound on distance, not an
ANI of zero — moot here, since all 79 have a reported neighbour.

## Species scope — a correction, stated precisely

The P1.11 confirmatory cohort is 79/79 *Escherichia coli*. It would be wrong to read that as the
programme being E. coli-only, and the distinction is per model:

| | Development / training | Prospective validation | Validated claim |
|---|---|---|---|
| **v1.1** | DEV_250: **7 genera**, *Enterococcus*-dominated (*Enterococcus* 157, *Klebsiella* 29, *Enterobacter* 29, *Escherichia* 15, *Citrobacter* 14, *Serratia* 5, *Pluralibacter* 1) | E. coli (P1.9 n=8, P1.10 n=39, P1.11 n=79) | **species-shifted generalisation**: E. coli is only 15/250 of its development data |
| **v1.2-General** | P1.10: 39 isolates, 3,811 contigs, **E. coli only** | E. coli (P1.11 n=79) | **E. coli-specific** |

The P1.10 cohort contract records this in its own words: *"Species scope is E. coli only, whereas
the development collection is Enterococcus-dominated (E. coli 15/250)."*

**What this licenses and what it does not.** The cross-genus evidence belongs to **v1.1**, whose
evaluation on E. coli is a genuine out-of-development-distribution test. It does **not** transfer to
v1.2-General, the principal surveillance model, which was trained and validated entirely within
E. coli. No validated performance claim outside *E. coli* may be made for v1.2-General.
