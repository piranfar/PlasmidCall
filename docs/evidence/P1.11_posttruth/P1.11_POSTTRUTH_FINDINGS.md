## 2. Cohort independence — quantitative, not merely procedural

MLST is **not available**: no MLST caller is installed on the execution host and none exists in the
frozen image set. Installing one after truth access would alter the frozen execution environment.
Lineage-stratified performance therefore cannot be computed, and is not estimated or substituted.

All 79 isolates are *Escherichia coli*, so the prespecified species stratum ("≥10 isolates with both
truth classes") yields exactly one group, identical to the whole cohort.

This is a property of the **P1.11 cohort**, not of the programme: v1.1's development collection
spans seven genera and is *Enterococcus*-dominated, so its evaluation here is species-shifted.
v1.2-General, by contrast, was trained on E. coli (P1.10) and validated on E. coli (P1.11).

Genomic distances were **recovered** from the pre-truth skani screen (skani 0.2.2, `--min-af 15`),
not regenerated, and the independence threshold (ANI ≥ 0.995) was reused unchanged.

- Zero retained isolates carry a near-identical development neighbour — but this is a *design*
  consequence of declustering before truth existed, not a measurement. The measurement is:
- **Every one of the 79 isolates has a measurable nearest development/consumed genome, and every one
  falls below the frozen independence threshold.** Maximum external ANI observed: **99.48 %**
  (median 99.08, IQR 98.91–99.20, minimum 98.07). None reaches 99.5 %.
- Maximum ANI between any two retained isolates: **99.45 %** — the cohort is also internally distinct.
- Performance does not track proximity to development data:

| Max ANI to development data | Isolates | Contigs | Selective PPV | Selective recall |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 98.0–<99.0 % | 30 | 2,970 | 0.9514 | 0.7686 |
| 99.0–<99.5 % | 49 | 4,274 | 0.9752 | 0.7856 |
| ≥99.5 % | 0 | 0 | — | — |

Spearman ρ between per-isolate accuracy and maximum external ANI = **0.12** over 79 isolates
(descriptive; no hypothesis test was prespecified). There is no evidence that proximity to the
development data inflates the result.

*Caveat, stated because it matters:* skani reports a pair only above its `--min-af 15` floor. An
isolate with no reported neighbour would be a lower bound on distance, not an ANI of zero. In this
cohort the question is moot — all 79 have a reported neighbour.

## 3. Comparison with the twelve-tool panel

This is the result that needs stating precisely, because a naïve F1 ranking would mislead.

**Four tools achieve a higher selective F1 than PlasmidCall v1.2-General:** PlaScope (0.9278),
gplas2 (0.9038), PlasmidEC (0.8925), Plasmer (0.8773), versus v1.2-General's 0.8626. That is
reported plainly and is not explained away.

The reason PlasmidCall is nevertheless the operative method is the estimand the programme declared
in advance — precision-first localisation:

- **Exactly one of the twelve tools reaches PPV ≥ 0.95: PlasmidFinder, at 0.9831 — with recall
  0.2009.** PlasmidCall v1.2-General reaches PPV 0.9662 at recall 0.7792, i.e. **3.9× the recall of
  the only comparably precise tool.**
- In the prespecified paired common-support comparisons, v1.2-General's PPV advantage is positive
  and its 95% CI excludes zero against **11 of the 12 tools**. Against the twelfth (PlasmidFinder)
  it gives up 0.0183 PPV [−0.0391, −0.0006] to gain **0.5793 recall** [0.5280, 0.6251].
- The higher-F1 tools reach that F1 by accepting materially lower precision: PlaScope 0.9221,
  gplas2 0.9296, PlasmidEC 0.9098, Plasmer 0.8134. None satisfies a PPV ≥ 0.95 requirement.

Two tools illustrate the failure mode PlasmidCall exists to avoid: PLASMe reaches recall 0.9818 at
PPV 0.4110, and geNomad recall 0.6882 at PPV 0.4527 — on this cohort, more than half of what they
call plasmid is chromosomal.

Bootstrap probabilities in Table 3 are **descriptive**, not frequentist p-values, and carry no
multiplicity correction across the twelve-comparison panel.

## 3b. Relation to prior work, and an independent reproduction of its central ranking

**A comparable benchmark was published before this work and must be credited.** Teixeira et al.
(Broad Institute and TU Delft) benchmarked 12 plasmid-detection tools and four plasmid-reconstruction
tools across Enterobacterales and *Enterococcus*, including poorly characterised species from
non-human hosts, using high-quality assemblies as ground truth:

> Teixeira M, Souque C, Worby CJ, Shea T, Commins N, Smith JT, Miklos AM, Abeel T, Earl AM,
> Manson AL. *Circling in on plasmids: benchmarking plasmid detection and reconstruction tools
> for short-read data from diverse species.* Briefings in Bioinformatics 2025;26(6):bbaf589.
> doi:10.1093/bib/bbaf589. Preprint: doi:10.1101/2025.07.28.667252 (2025-08-05).

Their headline finding is that **Plasmer, PlasmidEC, PlaScope and gplas2 were the highest-scoring
detection tools**, and that the two major determinants of detection accuracy were representation in
plasmid databases and assembly contiguity.

**Our results reproduce that ranking on a disjoint evaluation cohort — with one important
qualification.** The four highest-F1 comparators here are PlaScope (0.9278), gplas2 (0.9038),
PlasmidEC (0.8925) and Plasmer (0.8773): the same four, from the 79-isolate P1.11 cohort, which is
disjoint from their 250 isolates both by identifier (0 shared BioSamples) and by sequence (maximum
ANI 99.48 %, below the frozen 99.5 % threshold), using a different truth pipeline and assembler.

**The qualification, stated because it is material.** The development collection used to build
PlasmidCall v1.1 — DEV_250 — **is** the Teixeira et al. isolate collection. All 250 BioSample
accessions match exactly, as do the genus composition and the internal identifiers
(`docs/evidence/P1.9_development_accession_recovery_receipt_v2.md`). So the agreement is a genuine
cross-*cohort* reproduction at the evaluation level, but it is **not** independent in every
respect: the tool panel is the same, and v1.1's development data is their benchmark set. It should
be described as cross-cohort corroboration, never as a fully independent replication.

**What this work adds beyond that benchmark.** The tool comparison here is therefore *confirmatory
of prior work*, not novel. The contribution that remains specific to this work is:

1. **A trained meta-classifier over the full panel.** Teixeira et al. compared existing tools; they
   did not construct one. The only established ensemble in this space, PlasmidEC, combines three
   binary classifiers. PlasmidCall v1.2-General is fitted over the categorical calls of all twelve.
2. **A precision-first deployment framing.** No prior benchmark asks which tools clear a declared
   PPV floor. On this cohort exactly one of twelve does (PlasmidFinder, 0.9831) and it reaches
   recall 0.2009; v1.2-General clears the same floor at 3.9× that recall.
3. **Prospective freezing.** Sealed cohort, thresholds declared before truth access, predictions
   frozen and hashed before truth acquisition, and analysis scripts that refuse to run unless they
   re-derive the confirmatory contingency table exactly.
4. **ARG localisation and surveillance yield**, empirical score calibration, copy-number
   stratification and replicon-level recovery — none of which that benchmark reports.

**Where the prior benchmark is stronger.** It evaluates across Enterobacterales *and* enterococci,
whereas the confirmatory cohort here is single-species. It also covers plasmid *reconstruction*,
which is out of scope here. Both points are limitations of this work, not of theirs.

**Provenance disclosure.** Because DEV_250 is their isolate collection, the development set must be
cited as theirs in any manuscript. The P1.11 validation cohort is disjoint from it by identifier and
by sequence, and no P1.11 truth or metadata entered model development — but the relationship must be
stated rather than left for a reader to discover.

## 4. ARG localisation and surveillance yield

Of 271 ARG-bearing contigs in the frozen prediction table, **218** are ≥1 kb *and* carry a definitive
truth label; those 218, across 63 isolates, form the evaluable set. 166 are truth-plasmid, 52
truth-chromosome. Determinants are AMRFinderPlus core AMR calls; efflux is excluded.

| Method | Plasmid-borne ARG contigs recovered (of 166) | Chromosomal ARG contigs mislocalised to plasmid (of 52) |
|---|---|---|
| PLASMe | 166 (100 %) | 43 |
| Plasmer | 166 (100 %) | 36 |
| PlasmidEC | 163 (98 %) | 36 |
| PlaScope | 162 (98 %) | 32 |
| **PlasmidCall v1.2-General** | **148 (89 %)** | **18** |
| geNomad | 113 (68 %) | 28 |
| PlasmidFinder | 26 (16 %) | 3 |
| **PlasmidCall router** | **92 (55 %)** | **3** |

PlasmidCall v1.2-General on ARG contigs: selective PPV 0.8916 [0.8166, 0.9542], recall 0.8916
[0.8041, 0.9623]. The tools that recover every plasmid-borne ARG contig do so by calling 62–83 % of
chromosomal ARG contigs plasmid as well.

Clinically focused determinants (counts are contigs, not unique genes): carbapenemase 18 contigs,
all 18 truth-plasmid, 17 recovered by v1.2-General; ESBL/related β-lactamase 52 contigs, 36
truth-plasmid, 34 recovered; *mcr* colistin 7 contigs, 6 truth-plasmid, 5 recovered; aminoglycoside
including 16S methylase 68 contigs, 54 truth-plasmid, 50 recovered; PMQR fluoroquinolone 17 contigs,
13 truth-plasmid, 11 recovered.

**A finding about the frozen router that should not be buried.** The router routes **all and only**
ARG-bearing contigs to v1.1 — verified exactly: 271 contigs routed to v1.1, 271 ARG-bearing, the two
sets identical, and on those rows the router's call equals v1.1's call in 271/271 cases. Because v1.1
is far more conservative, **on the precise population that matters most for surveillance the router
recovers 92 of 166 plasmid-borne ARG contigs (55 %) where v1.2-General recovers 148 (89 %)** — 56
localisations lost. The router's superior whole-cohort PPV (0.9801 vs 0.9662) is purchased largely
there. This is an exploratory observation about a frozen artefact, not a proposal to change it; any
change would require its own prospective amendment.

*Framing:* this is a localisation and surveillance-yield analysis. **No claim of biological novelty
is made.** Establishing novelty would require a separate investigation against a plasmid reference
database, which was not performed.

## 5. Panel disagreement and model value-added

Disagreement is binary Shannon entropy over the plasmid/chromosome vote split with abstentions
excluded — defined from prediction-side artefacts only, never from truth.

| Panel plasmid votes | Contigs | Truth-positive | v1.2 PPV | v1.2 recall |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | 5,731 | 29 | — (no positive call) | 0.0000 |
| 3–5 | 339 | 90 | 0.9259 | 0.2778 |
| 6–8 | 253 | 210 | 0.9691 | 0.4476 |
| 9–12 | 921 | 880 | 0.9671 | 0.9352 |

PlasmidCall makes **no** plasmid call in the 0–2 stratum, missing all 29 truth-positives there; its
recall is concentrated where the panel already leans plasmid. Recall in the intermediate strata
(0.28, 0.45) is where the 267 false negatives live.

**A simple majority vote outperforms v1.2-General on F1, and this is reported plainly:** majority
vote PPV 0.9269, recall 0.8809, F1 0.9033, versus 0.9662 / 0.7792 / 0.8626. On the 731
high-disagreement contigs (entropy ≥ 0.8) majority resolves 561 correctly against v1.2's 498.
v1.2-General corrects **96** contigs the majority rule got wrong, but introduces **168** errors
on contigs the majority rule got right — a net deficit of 72 contigs on raw accuracy.

The material qualification: **majority vote's PPV of 0.9269 does not meet the PPV ≥ 0.95 requirement
the programme declared, and v1.2-General's 0.9662 does.** Majority vote is also not a deployable
calibrated model — it emits no score, cannot abstain, and was never prespecified as a comparator. It
is a descriptive reference computed here, not a rule fitted or selected on P1.11.

PlasmidCall **never** overrides a unanimous panel: 0 contigs where the panel is unanimous and v1.2
disagrees.

## 6. Error taxonomy

All 300 errors (33 FP, 267 FN) are enumerated one row per error in
`POSTTRUTH/P1.11_error_taxonomy.tsv`, across 57 of 79 isolates.

Observations — **no causal explanation is asserted**:

- **Errors are overwhelmingly false negatives** (267 vs 33), consistent with a precision-first
  operating point.
- **Error rate falls sharply with contig length**: 0.0844 (1–<2 kb), 0.0887 (2–<5 kb), 0.0600
  (5–<10 kb), 0.0174 (≥10 kb).
- **False negatives concentrate in panel disagreement**: 224 of 267 FNs sit at entropy ≥ 0.8, versus
  9 of 33 FPs. False positives sit mostly in the moderate band.
- **Truth quality does not explain the errors.** All 300 errors fall in the `clear` margin category —
  none sits near the truth decision boundary. Every FN is a `plasmid_fraction>=0.80` truth call and
  every FP a `chromosome_fraction>=0.80` call; these are unambiguous truth assignments.
- **The GenBank fallback did not degrade truth.** Error rate on GenBank-sourced isolates is **0.0277**
  versus **0.0455** on RefSeq-sourced — lower, not higher.
- 9 errors occur where **every available tool also erred**; these are panel-wide failures, not
  model-specific ones.
- ARG-bearing contigs are over-represented among FPs (18 of 33) relative to their 3 % share of the
  cohort.

## 6b. Copy number and replicon size explain where the recall deficit lives — EXPLORATORY

Produced after the accepted set was first frozen, so unambiguously post-hoc. Promotion into the
main record changed where these live, not their evidence tier.

**Copy number.** `coverage_ratio` = contig assembly depth ÷ median depth of that isolate's own
≥10 kb truth-chromosome contigs — a standard per-isolate plasmid-copy-number *proxy*, not a
measured copy number. Truth-plasmid contigs sit at median 1.574× (IQR 1.13–2.48) against
truth-chromosome 1.005× (IQR 0.95–1.08), so the proxy behaves as expected.

Recall rises monotonically with it:

| Copy-number proxy | Contigs | Truth-positive | Recall |
|---|---|---|---|
| <0.5× (sub-chromosomal) | 89 | 56 | 0.536 |
| 0.5–<1.5× (~single copy) | 5,965 | 504 | 0.688 |
| 1.5–<4× | 811 | 479 | 0.848 |
| 4–<15× | 289 | 85 | 0.882 |
| **≥15× (high copy)** | 90 | 85 | **0.988** |

Missed plasmid contigs have median ratio 1.176 (IQR 0.92–1.64); recovered ones 1.67 (IQR
1.24–2.64). Probability a randomly chosen missed contig has a lower copy proxy than a recovered
one: 0.689 (0.5 = no relationship; descriptive, no prespecified test).

The recall deficit is therefore not diffuse. It concentrates on single-copy and
sub-chromosomal-coverage plasmid contigs — the ones most chromosome-like in every signal available
to a classifier. On high-copy plasmids PlasmidCall recovers 98.8%.

**Replicon size.** Replicons grouped by `best_plasmid_hit`; size approximated by total assembled bp
assigned, a **lower bound** on true replicon length.

| Replicon size (assembled bp) | Replicons | Contigs | Contig-level recall |
|---|---|---|---|
| <10 kb | 148 | 150 | 0.933 |
| 10–<50 kb | 48 | 102 | 0.824 |
| 50–<100 kb | 79 | 418 | 0.766 |
| 100–<200 kb | 48 | 443 | 0.754 |
| ≥200 kb | 6 | 96 | 0.667 |

Median replicon 31.2 kb, p75 84.1 kb, max 248 kb. The ≥200 kb stratum rests on 6 replicons and is
indicative only. The decline is consistent with the independent observation that large
extrachromosomal replicons converge toward chromosomal composition as they grow, i.e. a
mechanistic reason large plasmids are intrinsically harder rather than a defect specific to this
model.

## 6c. Replicon-level recovery — the unit surveillance actually uses — EXPLORATORY

Per-contig recall (0.7792) answers "what fraction of plasmid contigs were flagged". For
surveillance the operative question is whether the plasmid was found at all, because one flagged
contig triggers follow-up. Across **329 plasmid replicons**:

| Outcome | Replicons | Fraction |
|---|---|---|
| At least one contig recovered | 312 | **0.948** |
| Fully recovered (every contig) | 241 | 0.733 |
| ≥50% of assembled bp recovered | 296 | 0.900 |
| Missed entirely | 17 | 0.052 |

Median per-replicon bp recovery is 1.00 — half of all plasmid replicons are recovered completely by
base pairs.

| | n | Fully recovered | Any contig recovered | Missed entirely |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Single-contig replicons | 197 | 181 (0.919) | 181 (0.919) | 16 |
| Multi-contig replicons | 132 | 60 (0.455) | 131 (0.992) | 1 |

**Two caveats travel with these numbers and must not be separated from them:**

1. "At least one contig recovered" is a **weaker** criterion than complete recovery and is
   mechanically easier to satisfy when a replicon is split across many contigs — a 10-contig
   replicon gets ten chances. The 0.992 figure for multi-contig replicons is partly that artefact,
   which is precisely why the stricter 0.455 sits beside it rather than instead of it.
2. Replicon assignment depends on `best_plasmid_hit`, so replicons sharing sequence may be merged
   or split relative to biological reality.

**The 0.948 replicon-level figure must never be presented as the prespecified recall estimand.**
The confirmatory recall remains 0.7792 [0.7353, 0.8239] per contig. Reporting 0.948 without 0.733
beside it would misrepresent the result.

## 8. Runtime and computational cost

**CPU time and peak memory are `not recorded`.** The frozen receipts capture tool, sample, exit code,
status, start/end UTC, wall seconds, image, image ID, docker command and mounts — no CPU or RSS
measurement was taken. This is stated rather than estimated. The frozen `docker run` declared
`--cpus=8 --memory=24g`; CPU-hours below are an **allocation-based upper bound, not a measurement**.

1,027 executions (13 components × 79 isolates). Summed wall time **16.6 h**; 132.6 CPU-hours at the
declared allocation. The panel ran concurrently, so elapsed time was lower.

Median wall seconds per isolate: Plasmer 159, geNomad 120, PLASMe 111, PlasmidEC 98, gplas2 53
(max 629), RFPlasmid 54, HyAsP 52, Platon 38, plASgraph2 8, PlasmidFinder 6, AMRFinderPlus 4,
PlaScope 1, MOB-recon 2.

**Two runtime figures require annotation or they mislead:**

- **MOB-recon shows 49 not-OK receipts.** A truth-blind provenance audit established that **47 of
  those executions completed and produced valid output behind a stale `FAILED` receipt**. Reconciled
  contig-level provenance on the evaluated set is 2,595 `OK` / 4,553 `RECOVERED_OK` / 96 `FAILED`.
  The raw receipt status is reported unaltered; the reconciled state is what the evaluation used.
- Genuine operational failures across the panel: 2 gplas2, 4 PlasmidFinder, 2 MOB-recon — all
  `rc=1 after 3 attempts`, preserved, not retried into success.

PlasmidCall's own scoring cost is negligible beside the panel it consumes, so its marginal runtime is
not separately meaningful; the honest cost statement is the full-panel total.

## 9. Limitations

1. **Species scope, stated precisely.** The P1.11 confirmatory cohort is 79/79 *Escherichia coli*,
   so the prespecified species stratification is degenerate here. The programme as a whole is **not**
   E. coli-only, and the distinction matters per model:
   - **v1.1** was developed on the DEV_250 internal collection, which spans **seven genera** and is
     *Enterococcus*-dominated (*Enterococcus* 157, *Klebsiella* 29, *Enterobacter* 29,
     *Escherichia* 15, *Citrobacter* 14, *Serratia* 5, *Pluralibacter* 1). Its evaluation on E. coli
     in P1.9, P1.10 and P1.11 is therefore a genuine **species-shifted generalisation test** —
     E. coli is only 15/250 of its development data. The P1.10 contract records this explicitly.
   - **v1.2-General** was trained on the 39-isolate P1.10 cohort (3,811 contigs), which is E. coli
     only, and validated on the 79-isolate E. coli P1.11 cohort. Its **validated** claim is
     therefore E. coli-specific.

   Nothing here establishes validated performance for v1.2-General outside *E. coli*. The
   cross-genus evidence belongs to v1.1, not to the principal model.
2. **No MLST.** Lineage structure within the cohort is unmeasured; the leakage audit rests on ANI,
   not on sequence type.
3. **v1.1 is a validated beta, not an independently validated model.** Its 0.9524 standard threshold
   was chosen after P1.10 results were seen. Only v1.2-General's 0.9285 and the frozen router carry
   a genuinely prospective operating point on P1.11.
4. **Recall is the weak axis.** 267 false negatives, concentrated in short contigs and in panel
   disagreement. Four comparator tools achieve higher F1 by trading precision for recall.
5. **No threshold exploration on P1.11.** The precision–recall frontier and the 0.98 crossover were
   deliberately omitted; locating them on this cohort would be threshold optimisation on the
   confirmatory data. They stand only where they were derived, in development.
6. **Truth is reference-alignment-based**, with 120 ambiguous and 152 unmapped contigs excluded, and
   10,852 contigs below the 1 kb floor never evaluated. Conclusions apply to ≥1 kb contigs with a
   confident reference assignment.
7. **Bootstrap probabilities are descriptive.** No multiplicity correction is applied across the
   comparison panel, and overlapping CIs are not treated as proof of no difference.
8. **Post-truth analyses cannot be prespecified.** Sections 2, 4, 5, 6 and the strata are
   EXPLORATORY. They generate hypotheses; they do not test them.

## 10. What is deliberately not claimed

- No claim that PlasmidCall is the best plasmid classifier by F1 — it is not, on this cohort.
- No claim of biological or epidemiological novelty for any ARG–plasmid association.
- No claim that the result generalises beyond *E. coli*, beyond short-read Unicycler assemblies, or
  below 1 kb.
- No claim that the panel is saturated, that performance has reached a ceiling, or that the
  evaluation is confirmatory for anything beyond the four frozen operating points.
- No causal explanation of any error stratum.
