# Internal-consistency review of the prior benchmark

**Subject:** Teixeira M, Souque C, Worby CJ, Shea T, Commins N, Smith JT, Miklos AM, Abeel T,
Earl AM, Manson AL. *Circling in on plasmids: benchmarking plasmid detection and reconstruction
tools for short-read data from diverse species.* Briefings in Bioinformatics 2025;26(6):bbaf589.
doi:10.1093/bib/bbaf589.

**Reviewed:** 2026-08-22, against the published PDF and the full supplementary workbook
(sha256 `3ca2a224e20c3d793904e55e845b2bd49a936832b9d9e2c92cd1b01d6755f2c6`).

**Purpose.** This project's development collection is that paper's isolate set and this project
reproduces its tool ranking, so its internal consistency bears directly on work here. Every
checkable claim was recomputed from the authors' own per-contig table (ST23, 12,795 rows).

**Overall assessment: the paper holds up.** The central claims reproduce from the authors' own
data. The issues below are a small number of specific numerical discrepancies and some
under-specified denominators — not a challenge to the conclusions.

---

## 1. Claims that reproduce exactly

| Claim in text | Recomputed from ST23 | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| "few plasmid (5.6%) contigs were correctly assigned by all tools" | 154 / 2,734 = **5.63 %** | ✅ exact |
| "20 plasmidic *Enterococcus* contigs were misclassified by all tools" | **20** | ✅ exact |
| "median of two … plasmids per isolate for enterococci" | median **2** | ✅ exact |
| Genus composition of the 250 isolates | matches ST1 exactly | ✅ |
| Highest Enterobacterales F1 = PlaScope | matches ST5 | ✅ |

## 2. Verified discrepancy: "just two chromosomal Enterobacterales"

> "just two chromosomal Enterobacterales and 20 plasmidic Enterococcus contigs were misclassified
> by all tools"

Recomputing from ST23, **four** Enterobacterales chromosomal contigs were called `plasmid` by all
twelve tools:

| Sample | SR contig | Calls |
|---|---|---|
| MGH43 | 35 | plasmid × 12 |
| MGH97 | 20 | plasmid × 12 |
| MGH97 | 24 | plasmid × 12 |
| UCICRE123 | 21 | plasmid × 12 |

This is not a definitional artefact. Every one is a unanimous opposite *call* with no abstentions,
so the count is 4 under either a strict reading (opposite call only) or a lenient one (anything but
correct). By contrast the companion figure of 20 for *Enterococcus* holds only under the lenient
reading — strictly it is 11 — so the two halves of the same sentence use different conventions,
and the Enterobacterales half is wrong under both.

Consequence: minor. It affects one descriptive sentence, not any performance metric.

---

## 3. Plasmid-count denominators cannot be reconciled from the paper

Four different totals appear for the same quantity:

| Source | Enterobacterales | *Enterococcus* |
|---|---|---|
| Main text ("the truth assemblies represented …") | **171** | **304** |
| ST4 (clusters × mean plasmids per cluster: 45×3.289, 78×4.026) | **148** | **314** |
| ST6 ("Number of plasmids") | **148** | **305** |
| Recomputed from ST23 (distinct sample + hybrid contig, truth = plasmid) | 366 all / **171** complete-assembly only | **324** |

The text's 171 matches a recount restricted to complete hybrid assemblies, so that figure is
traceable. The others are not: ST4 and ST6 agree at 148 for Enterobacterales but disagree for
*Enterococcus* (314 vs 305), and neither matches the 324 plasmids that actually carry at least one
short-read contig in ST23.

These may well be legitimately different subsets — plasmids in clusters, plasmids with an evaluable
contig, plasmids in complete assemblies. **The paper does not define which denominator each table
uses**, so a reader cannot reconcile them, and per-plasmid rates in ST6 cannot be independently
checked. This is the most substantive reporting issue found.

A related sentence is ambiguous in a way that matters:

> "49.7% (n = 314) of *Enterococcus* and 30.4% (n = 227) of Enterobacterales plasmids were fully
> captured in a circular contig"

Under the usual convention n is the count matching the percentage, which would imply totals of
~632 and ~747 — inconsistent with every figure above. Read as denominators, n = 314 and n = 227
conflict with ST6's 305 and 148. Either reading fails to reconcile.

## 4. Small numerical drift

> "Most chromosomal (60.3%) … contigs were correctly assigned by all tools"

Recomputed: 6,049 / 10,061 = **60.1 %**. A 0.2-point difference, consistent with a slightly
different denominator or rounding. Immaterial, noted only for completeness.

---

## 5. Text–table mismatch on the *Enterococcus* headline

> "Enterobacterales plasmids were easier to detect … (highest F1 score of 93.5% versus **84.2%**)"

ST5 gives, for *Enterococcus*, Plasmer **85.2** and PlaScope **84.3**. The value 84.2 appears
nowhere. The paper separately and correctly states "Plasmer, the best tool for the enterococci", so
the comparison sentence appears to quote PlaScope's *Enterococcus* score — mis-transcribed by 0.1 —
while calling it the highest, which it is not.

Consequence: minor; the qualitative claim (Enterobacterales easier) is amply supported.

---

## 6. Design points — not errors, but they bound what the results mean

These are not inconsistencies. They are properties a reader should know, and they matter for
interpreting any comparison against this benchmark.

**6.1 The ground truth is self-referential.** Truth is a hybrid assembly built from the *same reads*
as the short-read assembly being judged. Truth and test therefore share sequencing errors,
contamination and coverage biases. This is common practice and is not a flaw, but it is a
systematically more forgiving standard than truth from an independently deposited reference genome
for the same BioSample. Any cross-study comparison should treat the two designs as measuring
slightly different things.

**6.2 The hardest contigs are removed from every denominator.** "Contigs mapping equally well to a
hybrid-assembly chromosome and plasmid contig were excluded from downstream analyses." That is a
defensible choice, but **the number excluded is not reported**, so the amount by which every tool's
precision and recall is thereby raised cannot be estimated. Reporting that count would cost nothing
and would materially help reuse.

**6.3 Truth for incomplete assemblies was assigned manually, using read coverage.** For the 45
Enterobacterales assemblies that were not fully circular, "we inspected each contig manually …
including looking at its BLAST hit … and the relative plasmid copy number indicated by read
coverage." Two consequences: the labels are not reproducible from the deposited data, and copy
number is entangled with truth assignment — which is worth knowing given that copy number predicts
detectability.

**6.4 The twelve tools are not twelve independent methods.** PlasmidEC is by the paper's own Table 1
an ensemble of Platon, RFPlasmid and PlaScope, and gplas2 consumes a detection tool's output. So
statistics of the form "correctly assigned by all tools" or "misclassified by all tools" are
computed over a set with built-in correlation. The paper notes PlasmidEC's composition and observes
that its results resemble PlaScope's, but the unanimity statistics are still presented as if over
independent methods.

**6.5 Different assembler versions for truth and test.** Hybrid truth assemblies used Unicycler
v0.4.4 with Pilon; the short-read assemblies under test used v0.5.0. Defensible, and probably
immaterial, but unremarked.

---

## 7. What could not be checked

Bootstrap confidence intervals in ST5 could not be reproduced: the per-isolate medians recomputed
here from ST23 differ from the published values by 3–4 points for several tools, which most likely
reflects an aggregation detail — which isolates enter, how isolates lacking plasmids are handled —
that the paper does not fully specify. **No claim is made that the published values are wrong.**
The point is that the aggregation is under-described, so the intervals are not independently
reproducible from the deposited data.

---

## 8. Summary

| Finding | Severity |
|---|---|
| "two" chromosomal Enterobacterales misclassified by all tools; the data give four | minor, verified |
| Plasmid-count denominators differ across text, ST4, ST6 and ST23 with no stated definitions | **moderate** — blocks independent verification of per-plasmid rates |
| "84.2%" matches no value in ST5 and is called highest when it is not | minor |
| 60.3% vs a recomputed 60.1% | negligible |
| Count of excluded ambiguous contigs not reported | **moderate** — bounds on every metric are unknown |
| ST5 aggregation under-specified, intervals not reproducible | moderate |
| Self-referential truth; manual curation using coverage; non-independent tools | design properties to disclose, not errors |

None of this undermines the paper's conclusions, which reproduce from its own data. The
recommendation a reviewer would make is to define the plasmid denominators explicitly, report the
number of excluded ambiguous contigs, correct the two/four count and the 84.2 figure, and specify
the bootstrap aggregation.
